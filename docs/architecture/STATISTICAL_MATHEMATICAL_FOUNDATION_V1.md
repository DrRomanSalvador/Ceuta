# CeutIA — Statistical and Mathematical Foundation V1

The interaction algebra alone is not sufficient for scientific-grade inference. A complex system can be represented correctly and still produce invalid conclusions if dependence, selection, measurement error, uncertainty propagation, multiplicity, temporal correlation or predictive calibration are mishandled.

CeutIA therefore treats statistical validity as a first-class architectural layer.

## 1. Probability space and estimands

Every quantitative claim must have an implicit or explicit estimand. The system must distinguish a descriptive functional of observed data from a parameter of a stochastic data-generating process and from a causal estimand.

No estimator is accepted without recording its target, population/time window, conditioning set, assumptions and uncertainty representation.

## 2. Dependence

IID is never the default for longitudinal or networked observations.

The implementation supports covariance matrices, dependence-aware effective sample size diagnostics and block bootstrap. Stronger dependence structures must remain representable rather than being compressed into an IID standard error.

## 3. Uncertainty propagation

For a differentiable functional g(theta), first-order propagation is:

`Var[g(theta)] ≈ grad(g)^T Sigma grad(g)`.

CeutIA must retain Sigma when covariance matters. Independent scalar error bars must never be combined as though covariance were zero without an explicit independence assumption.

Structural/model uncertainty is not exchangeable with measurement variance.

## 4. Robust statistics

Complex systems are exposed to outliers, heavy tails, regime changes and contamination. Median, MAD, quantiles, IQR and trimmed means are therefore available as robust descriptive primitives.

Robustness is not interpreted as proof that a model is correct; it is a property of an estimator under specified contamination/deviation structures.

## 5. Resampling

The system supports ordinary percentile bootstrap and block bootstrap. Block bootstrap is the minimum defensible primitive when temporal dependence is known or suspected.

Bootstrap intervals are not automatically promoted to finite-sample coverage guarantees. Coverage must be demonstrated for the relevant data-generating process.

## 6. Multiplicity

Repeated hypothesis testing across variables, locations, times, models or subsystems inflates false discoveries. Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery-rate control is available as an explicit multiple-testing layer.

Multiplicity correction is not interchangeable with replication or external validation.

## 7. Randomisation/permutation inference

Permutation tests are available where exchangeability under the null is scientifically defensible. Exchangeability must be treated as an assumption; temporal, clustered or network dependence can invalidate naive permutation.

## 8. Predictive evaluation

Probabilistic predictions require proper scoring rules and calibration, not only point-error metrics. CeutIA supports Brier score, logarithmic score and empirical-sample CRPS primitives.

Forecast calibration is a prospective property and must be measured on outcomes not used to construct the prediction.

## 9. Statistical versus causal inference

A small p-value is not causal evidence. A well-calibrated forecast is not a causal explanation. A statistically significant association does not establish mechanism.

Statistical inference, causal identification, causal estimation and decision analysis remain separate layers.

## 10. Selection and observation processes

Observed data are generated by both the underlying system and an observation mechanism. Missingness, censoring, truncation, selection, measurement change and availability therefore belong in the state/evidence model.

A distributional change in observations cannot be interpreted as a distributional change in the latent system until observation-process change has been considered.

## 11. Finite-sample and asymptotic discipline

Large-sample approximations are not treated as exact identities. Every asymptotic approximation must retain its conditions of validity. Small samples, weak identification, sparse events and rare regimes require explicit uncertainty or abstention rather than false precision.

## 12. Numerical integrity

Covariance objects are required to be symmetric and positive semidefinite. Invalid covariance structures are rejected before uncertainty propagation.

Numerical tolerances are implementation tolerances, not scientific evidence.

## 13. Interaction with complex-system dynamics

The statistical layer must operate jointly with the interaction algebra:

`observations → observation model → latent state → dynamic transition → interaction network → uncertainty propagation → inference → forecast/causal model → decision`.

At each transition, provenance, uncertainty, temporal resolution, dependence structure and epistemic status remain attached.

## 14. Remaining scientific frontier

This foundation is intentionally not declared complete merely because primitives exist. Further implementation remains necessary for:

- multivariate state-space filtering/smoothing;
- non-Gaussian and nonlinear filtering;
- hierarchical/multilevel models;
- spatial and spatiotemporal dependence;
- missing-data sensitivity analysis under MNAR;
- measurement-error models and latent-variable identification;
- clustered/longitudinal causal estimators;
- time-varying confounding and marginal structural models;
- sensitivity to unmeasured confounding;
- rare-event and tail inference;
- conformal and distribution-free predictive coverage where assumptions permit;
- Bayesian posterior and posterior-predictive model averaging;
- sequential inference and optional-stopping control;
- online change-point and regime inference;
- high-dimensional regularisation and multiplicity control;
- network/hypergraph statistical inference;
- spatial causal inference and interference;
- formal model checking and posterior/predictive calibration.

The purpose of this document is therefore to prevent premature claims of mathematical completeness while making the required standard explicit.
